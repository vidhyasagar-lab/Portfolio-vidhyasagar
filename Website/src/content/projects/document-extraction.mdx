## The constraint

An aggregate accuracy number is close to useless for an extraction system that feeds a downstream
process. If 6% of fields are wrong and they're indistinguishable from the correct 94%, a human has
to check all of them — and you've saved nobody any work.

The goal was not higher accuracy. It was **calibrated confidence**: being reliably right about
which fields to doubt.

## What I built

A pipeline that parses scanned documents to structured records against a Pydantic schema, and
emits a per-field confidence derived from three signals — token-level model probability, agreement
between two independent extraction passes at different temperatures, and whether the value
satisfies its schema constraint (dates in range, totals reconciling against line items).

Fields below threshold surface in a review queue with the source region highlighted. Everything
above it flows straight through.

## The decision I'd defend

**Two passes at different temperatures instead of one careful pass.**

Running the extraction twice costs roughly double, which was the obvious objection. But
disagreement between the passes turned out to be the single strongest predictor of an incorrect
field — much stronger than raw token probability, which is well-calibrated for fluency and poorly
calibrated for factual correctness. The doubled inference cost bought a signal that made the
review queue trustworthy, and the review time saved dwarfed it.

## What I'd change

The schema validation layer catches structural errors but has no notion of cross-document
consistency — a party name spelled two ways across a contract set passes cleanly. I'd add an
entity-resolution step across each batch and treat inconsistency as its own confidence signal.
